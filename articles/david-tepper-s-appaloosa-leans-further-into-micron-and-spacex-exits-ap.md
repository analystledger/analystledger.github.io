## Who they are

David Tepper founded Appaloosa Management in 1993, according to CNBC. He built his reputation during the financial crisis, when Appaloosa bought deeply depressed bank securities that later recovered strongly, a trade that is still among the most cited in hedge fund history. Tepper also owns the Carolina Panthers NFL team.

In 2019 Tepper said he would return most outside capital and turn Appaloosa into what is effectively a family office, keeping money for a small group of roughly 15 investors, CNBC and Bloomberg reported. At that time he managed about $14 billion, roughly 70% of it his own money. TIKR now puts firm-wide assets at about $20.4 billion. The disclosed US stock portfolio is a much smaller slice of that total.

## How they invest

Tepper is best described as an opportunistic, macro-aware investor. He looks for assets priced for disaster and buys when sentiment is weakest, whether in distressed debt, bank stocks or, more recently, out-of-favour technology and Chinese internet stocks. He is willing to concentrate heavily and to move fast: positions are often doubled, halved or sold outright within a quarter or two as his view of the economy, interest rates or policy changes.

His public commentary, typically on CNBC, has moved markets in the past because he tends to tie stock picks to a view on central banks and liquidity. The 13F book usually holds a few dozen names, with a handful of large bets at the top. In recent quarters it has leaned heavily toward semiconductors, cloud and AI-related companies.

## Latest disclosed moves

Appaloosa filed its 13F for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 on 16 November. TIKR reports US stock holdings of $7.56 billion across 24 positions.

- **Top holdings:** Micron, 1.25 million shares worth $1.33 billion (17.6%); Amazon, about 14.7% of the portfolio; SpaceX (SPCX), 3.88 million shares worth about $557 million (7.4%, fourth largest); Nvidia, 2.0 million shares worth $457 million (6.0%).
- **Added:** SpaceX up about 1,626% in shares from the 225,000 bought in the second quarter, making it the largest purchase of the period. Micron was up 275,000 shares (about 28%) and Nvidia up about 31%.
- **New positions:** Oracle (1.3 million shares, about $172 million), Applied Materials (225,000 shares, about $115 million) and a small PG&E stake (2.5 million shares, about $30.9 million). Appaloosa has held Oracle and Applied Materials in earlier periods.
- **Cut:** Alphabet down 850,000 shares to 1 million. NRG Energy, American Airlines and the iShares South Korea ETF were also reduced.
- **Exited:** Advanced Micro Devices (197,500 shares), Broadcom (150,000 shares), Apple, Berkshire Hathaway, Goodyear, Qualcomm and Whirlpool.

## How to follow them quickly

- **Form 13F:** filed quarterly under Appaloosa LP within 45 days of quarter-end. It is the main public record of the stock portfolio.
- **Schedule 13D/13G:** filed if Appaloosa crosses 5% of a company. Since 2024 a 13D is due within five business days. Tepper rarely runs activist campaigns, so a passive 13G is more typical.
- **Form 4:** required within two business days only if Appaloosa becomes a 10% owner or insider, which is uncommon.
- **Media appearances:** Tepper's occasional television interviews, especially on CNBC, are often the quickest signal of a change in his market view.
- **EDGAR:** [Appaloosa LP filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001656456&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40) (CIK 0001656456).

## What the filings don't show

The 13F lists only US-listed long equities, certain ETFs and options, as of a date up to 45 days before release. Appaloosa has historically been active in corporate and sovereign debt, distressed credit and macro trades, and none of these appear. Neither do short positions, cash or most non-US listings. Options are reported at the value of the underlying shares, not at their cost, which can overstate exposure. Because Tepper trades quickly, a position shown at 30 September may already have been resized or sold by the time the filing is public. Copying the list therefore captures neither his timing nor his hedges.
