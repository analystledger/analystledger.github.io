## Who they are

Li Lu was born in China in 1966 and was a student leader during the 1989 Tiananmen Square protests. He later moved to the United States and earned a BA in economics, an MBA and a JD from Columbia University, all in 1996. He has said he was drawn to investing after hearing Warren Buffett speak at Columbia. He founded Himalaya Capital Management in 1997.

His best-known patron was Charlie Munger, who met him in 2003, invested family money with him and called him the "Chinese Warren Buffett." Munger reportedly said Li Lu was the only outside manager he had entrusted with his money. Li Lu is also known for introducing Chinese carmaker BYD to Munger and Berkshire Hathaway. Himalaya is a private partnership, and its returns are not publicly reported.

## How they invest

Li Lu follows a Buffett- and Munger-style value approach: he looks for durable businesses run by capable managers, buys when the price is well below his estimate of intrinsic value, and holds for years. The portfolio is unusually concentrated. Over recent quarters, three or four names have typically made up most of the reported US book. He invests in both US and Asian companies, so the 13F covers only part of his activity. In the second quarter of 2026 he made a large reshuffle, selling out of Bank of America, Occidental Petroleum, Moody's, S&P Global, MSCI and H&R Block while more than doubling PDD and adding to Berkshire Hathaway.

## Latest disclosed moves

Himalaya filed its 13F for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 on 16 November 2026. It reports about $3.78 billion across eight US-listed lines (seven companies), up about 2% from June.

- **Top holdings:** PDD Holdings about 24% (about 11.76 million ADS), Alphabet Class A about 23%, Alphabet Class C about 22%, Berkshire Hathaway Class B about 15%, East West Bancorp about 9%.
- **Added:** PDD, up 1 million ADS; Tencent Music Entertainment, more than doubled.
- **New positions:** none.
- **Cut or exited:** none.
- **Smaller holdings:** Crocs and Apple, unchanged.

Taken together, Alphabet's two share classes remain the largest single exposure at about 45% of reported value.

## How to follow them quickly

- **Form 13F:** quarterly, up to 45 days after quarter-end, under CIK 0001709323 on [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001709323&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40). This is the main window into the US portfolio.
- **Schedule 13D/13G:** required if a stake in a US-listed company passes 5%. A 13D is due within five business days (rule since 2024), and a passive 13G has longer deadlines. Himalaya's US stakes are usually below that level.
- **Form 4:** applies only above 10% ownership or for insiders, so it rarely applies here.
- **Talks and writing:** Li Lu does not publish regular shareholder letters. His views surface mainly through occasional lectures, interviews and writing, much of it in Chinese, including a 2020 book on value investing and China.

## What the filings don't show

The 13F is a delayed snapshot of US-listed long positions. It excludes Hong Kong, mainland Chinese and other non-US holdings (such as any shares of Hong Kong-listed BYD), as well as any shorts, bonds and cash. It does not reveal his cost basis or his reasons for a trade. With so few positions, each line is a large bet, so copying the portfolio means taking on concentrated single-stock and China-related risk, with a lag of up to six weeks.
