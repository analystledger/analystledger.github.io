## Who they are

Stanley Druckenmiller is a macro investor who ran Duquesne Capital Management for three decades before returning outside money in 2010. At the time of the closure, InvestmentNews reported that the fund managed about $12 billion, had averaged roughly 30% a year, and had never posted a losing calendar year. For part of that period he also managed money for George Soros's Quantum Fund, where he is widely credited with a central role in the 1992 bet against the British pound.

Since 2010 he has invested his own capital through Duquesne Family Office LLC. Because it manages more than $100 million in US-listed equities, the family office files a quarterly Form 13F with the SEC.

## How they invest

Druckenmiller is best known as a top-down macro trader: he forms a view on interest rates, currencies, liquidity and economic cycles, then expresses it across stocks, bonds, currencies and commodities. He is willing to concentrate heavily when conviction is high and to turn over the portfolio quickly when his view changes, so holding periods can be short.

The 13F reflects only one part of that toolkit, the US-listed equity and option book. Recent filings show a mix of large single-stock bets, sector themes (healthcare, semiconductors and storage, Argentina) and index or ETF call options. In public remarks reported by Institutional Investor in November 2026, he said he holds little net equity exposure, is focused on preserving capital, is short copper, and is shifting from AI infrastructure toward companies that use AI.

## Latest disclosed moves

Duquesne filed its 13F for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 on 16 November 2026. The reported total fell to about $4.47 billion across 65 lines, from about $5.21 billion across 95 lines at the end of June. Institutional Investor, counting common stocks only, put the figure at about $4.15 billion across 60 stocks.

- **Top holdings (share of reported value):** Natera about 29%, Seagate about 7.1% (more with call options), Amazon about 4.3%, Fox Corp about 4.3%, YPF about 4.0%.
- **New:** Newmont (380,400 shares), Barrick Mining (1,054,600) and Agnico Eagle (231,050), together about $129 million; also Western Digital, Palantir, AppLovin, SpaceX, Somnigroup (SGI) and Seagate call options.
- **Added:** Seagate shares up about 180% to 341,790; Unity Software up about 436%; Amazon up about 43%; Fox up about 38%.
- **Cut:** Taiwan Semiconductor ADS down about 94%, from 589,680 to 32,700 shares.
- **Exited:** Alphabet (336,300 Class A shares in June), United Airlines (794,795 shares), Sandisk, AMD, Cleveland-Cliffs, Southern Copper and Alcoa, plus several ETF call options.

## How to follow them quickly

- **Form 13F:** filed quarterly, up to 45 days after quarter-end. Search under CIK 0001536411 on [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001536411&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40).
- **Schedule 13D/13G:** required when a stake passes 5% of a company's shares. Since 2024, a 13D is due within five business days. Duquesne rarely crosses this threshold.
- **Form 4:** due within two business days for holders of more than 10% of a class or for insiders. This seldom applies to Druckenmiller.
- **Interviews and conference appearances:** these are often the earliest signal of his macro views, including positions that never appear in a 13F.

## What the filings don't show

A 13F is a snapshot taken up to six weeks before it becomes public, and Duquesne can trade actively in that gap. It lists long US-listed equities and options only. It omits short positions such as the copper short he described, Treasuries, currencies, futures, non-US listings and cash. Options appear at the notional value of the underlying shares, which can overstate economic exposure, and the reported total differs depending on whether those lines are counted. Copying a fast-moving macro trader from stale data carries real timing risk.
