## Who they are

Howard Marks is co-founder and co-chair of Oaktree Capital Management, which began in 1995 with strategies including high-yield bonds and opportunistic credit. Marks has invested in high-yield debt since 1978, according to Oaktree. He is widely known for his client memos, written since 1990 and read well beyond Oaktree's client base, and for books including *The Most Important Thing* and *Mastering the Market Cycle*.

Brookfield bought a majority of Oaktree in 2019 and agreed in October 2025 to buy the remaining roughly 26% for about $3 billion. Oaktree's website says Brookfield completed the full acquisition in 2026. Capital Brief reported that Marks will remain on Brookfield Corporation's board.

## How they invest

Oaktree is primarily a credit investor. It works in high-yield bonds, distressed debt, senior loans, private lending and structured credit, alongside real estate and some equity strategies. The firm describes its approach as opportunistic, value-oriented and focused on controlling risk. Its best-known style is buying debt of stressed or bankrupt companies when other lenders are forced to sell, which sometimes leaves Oaktree holding post-restructuring equity.

Marks's memos stress market cycles, investor psychology, and the idea that avoiding losses matters more than chasing gains. His most cited calls include warnings before the dot-com bust and the 2008 credit crisis, and a push to buy distressed debt in late 2008.

## Latest disclosed moves

The most recent public signals come from memos, not trades:

- **11 November 2026, "A layman thinks about AI":** Marks argues that AI's winners, moats and earnings are highly uncertain. He flags heavy AI-related borrowing and vendor-financed deals as risks lenders must watch, while cautioning that waiting for clarity also has costs.
- **22 September 2026, "Shall We Repeal the Laws of Economics, Part III":** he links rising long-term rates to deficits, above-target inflation and capital demand, and says large-scale selling of US stocks is not the right response.
- **9 April 2026, private credit memo:** he describes looser lending standards in direct lending, with stress concentrated in software loans.

For the quarter ended 30 September 2026, Oaktree filed a Form 13F-NT notice on 17 November 2026. A notice means its holdings are reported in other managers' filings, including Brookfield Corporation's. Its last full 13F, filed 14 August 2026 for the June quarter, listed 145 positions worth about $5.31 billion. Moves in that filing included:

- **New:** Matthews International, MakeMyTrip, Surgery Partners, Sea Ltd.
- **Added:** Oaktree Specialty Lending (about 6 million shares), Liberty Latin America.
- **Cut:** Garrett Motion (7 million shares), Core Scientific.
- **Exited:** Nokia, Altice USA, Petrobras.

## How to follow them quickly

- **Memos:** published on Oaktree's and Brookfield's websites, often the fastest window into Marks's thinking.
- **Form 13F:** quarterly within 45 days. Check Oaktree under CIK 0000949509 on [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0000949509&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40) to see whether it files a full report or a notice, and if it is a notice, review Brookfield Corporation's 13F.
- **Schedule 13D/13G:** Oaktree often crosses 5% in restructured companies. A 13D is due within five business days (rule since 2024).
- **Form 4:** within two business days when Oaktree owns more than 10% of a company or holds board seats.
- **Fund reports:** listed vehicles such as Oaktree Specialty Lending file their own quarterly reports.

## What the filings don't show

Most of Oaktree's capital sits in bonds, loans and private assets, none of which appear in a 13F, so the equity filing captures only a small, often incidental slice of the firm. The 13F is also delayed by up to 45 days, lists longs only, omits shorts, non-US securities and cash, and shows options at notional value. Many listed stakes come from debt restructurings rather than deliberate stock picks, so copying them can be misleading.
