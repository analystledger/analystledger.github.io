## Who they are

Warren Buffett built Berkshire Hathaway from a struggling textile maker into a conglomerate that owns insurers, a railroad, utilities and dozens of operating businesses, alongside one of the largest stock portfolios in the world. In 2024 it became the first non-technology US company valued above $1 trillion, according to the Associated Press.

The leadership handover is now complete. Greg Abel, who oversaw the non-insurance businesses as vice chairman from 2018, became chief executive at the start of 2026. On 18 September 2026 Buffett, aged 96, stepped down as chairman, a post he had held since 1970. His son Howard Buffett, a director since 1993, became chairman, while Warren Buffett took the title of chairman emeritus and remains on the board, the AP reported. Kiplinger reports that Abel now oversees the equity portfolio.

## How they invest

Berkshire's approach, shaped by Buffett over six decades, is to buy durable businesses with strong economics at sensible prices and hold them for years, ideally indefinitely. The firm prefers to buy whole companies, and uses the stock portfolio for minority stakes in businesses it understands, such as consumer brands, banks and Apple.

The portfolio is concentrated. Kiplinger estimates the top five US holdings account for about 69% of reported value, with Apple alone close to a quarter of the stock portfolio. Large cash reserves are a deliberate feature: Kiplinger puts cash at $357 billion at the end of the third quarter, down from a record $380 billion in the first quarter. Under Abel, Berkshire has leaned more on share buybacks, repurchasing $9.6 billion of its own stock in the third quarter, according to Kiplinger.

## Latest disclosed moves

Berkshire filed its 13F for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 on 16 November. It reported US-listed equities worth $304.2 billion, according to StockAnalysis and Morningstar. Morningstar, citing the 10-Q, says Berkshire was a net seller of $426 million in the quarter, with about $2.8 billion of sales against $2.4 billion of purchases.

- **Largest holding:** Apple, valued at $75.9 billion, share count unchanged.
- **New:** Bending Spoons (BSP), 20.7 million shares worth $689 million at quarter-end. Berkshire bought into the July IPO and agreed in October to invest a further $330 million at $33 a share, per BeBeez.
- **Added:** Delta Air Lines up 12% to 64.45 million shares ($5.38 billion); Lennar Class A roughly doubled to 26 million shares; a small add to Macy's.
- **Trimmed:** Bank of America down about 6% to 455.9 million shares ($24.8 billion), still a top-five holding; Capital One down 62%; VeriSign down 34%; Nucor down 15%; Kroger down 10%; DaVita trimmed slightly.
- **Exits:** None reported for the quarter.

## How to follow them quickly

- **Form 13F:** filed quarterly within 45 days of quarter-end; it is the main window into the US stock portfolio.
- **Schedule 13D/13G:** required when Berkshire crosses 5% of a company's voting shares. Since 2024 a 13D is due within five business days.
- **Form 4:** where Berkshire owns more than 10% of a US company, trades must be reported within two business days. This is how purchases and sales of large stakes surface well before the 13F.
- **10-Q and 10-K:** the quarterly reports show net stock purchases or sales, buybacks and cash, usually ahead of the 13F.
- **Annual letter and meeting:** the shareholder letter, published in late February, and the Omaha annual meeting in May.
- **EDGAR:** [Berkshire Hathaway filings](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=0001067983&type=&dateb=&owner=include&count=40) (CIK 0001067983).

## What the filings don't show

The 13F is a snapshot taken up to 45 days before it becomes public, and positions may have changed since. It lists only US-listed long equity positions. Berkshire's large Japanese trading-house stakes, other foreign holdings, Treasury bills and wholly owned businesses are not included. Morningstar puts total stock holdings, including foreign stakes, at $357.6 billion, well above the 13F figure. The SEC can also let Berkshire withhold a position from a filing for a time while it is still building it. A position copied from a filing is bought at today's price, not Berkshire's cost, and without its time horizon or tax position.
