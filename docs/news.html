Chipmakers give back Monday's surge as Nvidia drops 7–9% and Nasdaq slides about 2%

A day after Nvidia jumped about 31% on Jensen Huang's reinstatement as CEO, the stock fell roughly 7–9% intraday Tuesday, AMD lost about 9%, Micron and SK Hynix dropped more than 10% and SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital each fell 5% or more. Why it matters: The swing shows how crowded the AI hardware trade has become, with memory, storage and GPU names moving together on sentiment rather than company news. Wednesday's PCE inflation report and traders' high odds of another Fed rate hike remain the next tests for richly valued tech stocks.

High impact 2026-11-24 12:01 UTC · Earnings

Analog Devices beats on 42% growth, yet shares sink ~17% on softer guide and capex jump

Analog Devices posted record fiscal Q4 revenue of $4.37 billion, up 42% from a year earlier and ahead of the roughly $4.32 billion consensus, with adjusted EPS of $4.06 versus about $3.89 expected and up 80% year on year. It guided fiscal Q1 revenue to $4.4 billion ±$150 million but adjusted EPS to $3.82 ±$0.20, below the quarter just reported, and lifted fiscal 2027 capex to $1.6–1.9 billion from $0.7 billion; shares fell about 17–18% premarket and were down roughly 19% by midday.

Why it matters: The reaction is a read-through for analog and mixed-signal peers such as Texas Instruments, Microchip and NXP, where investors are weighing strong industrial and data-center demand against heavier spending and easing gross margins. Management said bookings still run above billings, so the open question is whether the capex build and the guided dip to about 74% gross margin prove temporary.

Sources: Analog Devices press release, Investing.com, Motley Fool via Yahoo Finance

Medium impact 2026-11-24 12:00 UTC · Earnings

Dick's jumps ~16% as Q3 profit tops forecasts and full-year EPS outlook moves higher

Dick's Sporting Goods reported Q3 net sales up 23% to $5.13 billion, helped by a full quarter of Foot Locker, and adjusted EPS of $1.94 against a consensus near $1.41, with comparable sales up 2.6% at the core Dick's business and 0.5% at Foot Locker. It raised its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $11.45–$12.30 from $11.00–$12.00, bought back $903 million of stock in the quarter, and its shares rose about 16% Tuesday morning.

Why it matters: The results inform the read on athletic retail and brand partners such as Nike, as Foot Locker posts its first slight comp gain under new ownership. Foot Locker remains loss-making, with 134 stores closed so far this year and up to $750 million in total restructuring charges planned.

Medium impact 2026-11-24 12:00 UTC · Earnings

Best Buy posts its strongest comps in five years at +6.1% and lifts full-year outlook

Best Buy's fiscal Q3 revenue rose 5.8% to $10.24 billion and comparable sales grew 6.1%, well above the roughly 2.3% analysts expected, while adjusted EPS of $1.62 beat the $1.49 consensus and rose from $1.40 a year earlier. The retailer raised fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $43.0–$43.3 billion and adjusted EPS to $6.90–$7.05 from $6.70–$6.90, and its shares rose about 5% before the open.

Why it matters: Gains in computing, mobile and services point to a hardware upgrade cycle that matters for suppliers such as Apple and HP heading into the holidays. Q4 comp guidance of 2–4% is more modest, and domestic margins were helped by $41 million of one-off tariff refunds.

Medium impact 2026-11-24 12:00 UTC · Earnings

Kohl's returns to sales growth and tops EPS forecasts, helped by one-time gains

Kohl's reported Q3 comparable sales up 1.2%, its first positive quarterly comp in a long stretch of declines, and adjusted EPS of $1.76 against Wall Street expectations near breakeven, with gross margin up 145 basis points to 41.0%. It raised its 2026 outlook to diluted EPS of $4.30–$4.50 and flat to +0.5% sales, and shares rose about 13% premarket and roughly 17% by midday.

Why it matters: A turn at Kohl's adds to evidence that middle-income shoppers are holding up for department stores such as Macy's heading into the holidays. Tariff refunds, a real estate gain and a tax benefit inflate both results and guidance, and management expects a more promotional fourth quarter.

High impact 2026-11-24 11:30 UTC · Earnings

Dollar Tree shoppers keep coming: traffic-led Q3 beat lifts full-year outlook

Dollar Tree's Q3 comparable sales rose 4.6% as store visits climbed 3.8%, lifting net sales 6.9% to $5.07 billion, and adjusted EPS of $1.16 cleared the $0.91 consensus despite a $0.42 hit from tariff-related reinvestment. The retailer raised its full-year adjusted EPS outlook to $8.05-$8.25, including about $0.65 from tariff refunds, and shares jumped about 11% in premarket trading.

Why it matters: Rising traffic at a discount chain is an early read on how budget-conscious households are spending into the holiday season, relevant for peers like Dollar General and Five Below. Part of the higher outlook rests on one-time tariff refunds, so the underlying earnings trend is less clear than the headline raise suggests.

High impact 2026-11-24 11:00 UTC · Mergers & acquisitions

Sanofi bets $41 billion on Insmed, paying a 46% premium in all-cash biotech deal

Sanofi agreed to buy Insmed for $175 per share in cash, valuing the drugmaker at about $41.0 billion and paying roughly 46% above its Nov. 18 close, the last price before deal reports surfaced. Sanofi will fund the purchase with debt and cash, expects it to add to business EPS by the end of 2028, and leaves its 2026 guidance unchanged; Insmed shares rose about 14% before Tuesday's open.

Why it matters: The deal shows big pharma paying up for late-stage growth as blockbusters such as Sanofi's Dupixent approach patent expiry, which keeps attention on other mid-cap biotechs. Closing, targeted for the first half of 2027, still needs Insmed shareholder approval and US and international antitrust clearance.

Sources: Insmed press release (SEC 8-K), Investing.com

High impact 2026-11-23 21:05 UTC · Earnings

HPE beats and raises, yet shares slide as AI server boom threatens margins

Hewlett Packard Enterprise grew fiscal Q4 revenue 50% to $14.5 billion, earned $1.43 a share on a non-GAAP basis against its own $1.20-$1.30 forecast, lifted its fiscal 2027 outlook to $5.05-$5.35 per share and added $5 billion to its buyback. Shares still fell roughly 10% to 12% before Tuesday's open after management said its Cloud & AI segment margin could ease to about 14% next year from 18.4% in Q4 as lower-margin AI systems make up more of sales.

Why it matters: The reaction puts AI hardware profitability, not just demand, under the spotlight, a question that also hangs over server peers such as Dell and Super Micro. How deep the squeeze runs depends on the timing and mix of AI system shipments, which HPE expects to tilt toward the second half of fiscal 2027.
