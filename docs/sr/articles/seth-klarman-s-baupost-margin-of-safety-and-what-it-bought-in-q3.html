Seth Klarman's Baupost: margin of safety, and what it bought in Q3

Autor: Analyst Ledger research desk · 2026-11-28 · 3 min čitanja

Baupost's third-quarter 13F shows new stakes in Honeywell Aerospace and SiteOne Landscape Supply and four full exits, including Norwegian Cruise Line and Liberty Global. Amazon remains the largest disclosed holding.

Who they are

Seth Klarman has run The Baupost Group, a Boston-based investment firm, since it was formed in May 1982, and serves as its chief executive and portfolio manager. Baupost manages money for individual and institutional clients. Reported assets under management vary by source: Harvard Kennedy School's profile puts them at about $26 billion, and a June 2026 Bloomberg Masters in Business episode at about $22 billion. The same episode cited annual net returns of roughly 20% over four decades, a figure that is reported rather than audited.

Klarman wrote Margin of Safety (1991), a book setting out his value-investing philosophy. He also edited the seventh edition of Graham and Dodd's Security Analysis, published in 2023.

How they invest

Baupost is a patient, contrarian value investor in the tradition of Benjamin Graham. Klarman's central idea is the margin of safety: buy only when the price is well below a conservative estimate of value, so that mistakes or bad luck do not cause permanent loss. Avoiding losses comes ahead of maximising gains.

In practice the firm works across asset classes. It buys public equities but has also made large commitments to distressed debt, real estate, private investments and other complex or out-of-favour situations where forced sellers create bargains. Baupost has been willing to hold substantial cash when it cannot find enough cheap assets, and to accept lagging returns in strong markets. Equity positions are typically held for several years, though the firm trims and exits steadily as prices approach its estimate of value.

Latest disclosed moves

Baupost filed its 13F for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 on 13 November. Reported US equities totalled about $5.05 billion, down 6.8% from about $5.42 billion in June, across 21 positions (23 in June), according to Rankia and Seeking Alpha.

  • Top holdings: Amazon 18.5% (3,743,854 shares, unchanged), Elevance Health 9.8%, Restaurant Brands International 9.5%, Genuine Parts 7.7%, Ferguson 6.9%. The top ten made up 77% of the portfolio.
  • New: Honeywell Aerospace (HONA), 1.13 million shares worth about $175 million; SiteOne Landscape Supply, 1.25 million shares.
  • Added: Axalta Coating Systems roughly doubled to 2.51 million shares; Herbalife, Genuine Parts, Ferguson, CME Group and Wesco each rose by about 8% to 11%.
  • Cut: Union Pacific down about a third to 795,400 shares; Alphabet Class C down about 31% to 949,930 shares; Molina Healthcare down 29%; Aon trimmed slightly.
  • Exited: Norwegian Cruise Line (7.73 million shares), Liberty Global (8.54 million Class C shares), Teleflex (1.4 million shares) and DNOW (2.79 million shares).

How to follow them quickly

  • Form 13F: filed quarterly within 45 days of quarter-end. It is the only regular public view of Baupost's US stock portfolio.
  • Schedule 13D/13G: filed when Baupost owns more than 5% of a company. Since 2024 a 13D is due within five business days. For a concentrated holder of smaller companies, these filings can appear between 13Fs.
  • Form 4: required within two business days where Baupost is a 10% owner or insider.
  • Investor letters: Klarman's annual letters go to clients only, but excerpts are often reported by the financial press. Public interviews are rare.
  • EDGAR: Baupost Group filings (CIK 0001061768).

What the filings don't show

The 13F covers only US-listed long equities and is published up to 45 days after the quarter ends. At about $5 billion, it is a minority of Baupost's assets, as Seeking Alpha notes. Its distressed debt, private deals, real estate, non-US holdings, cash and any hedges or shorts are not shown. A position that looks small or large in the 13F can therefore be misleading about its weight in the overall fund. Investors copying the list also miss Klarman's entry prices and the cash reserve that cushions his portfolio.
