Open and set up a trading account

A practical guide using Interactive Brokers (IBKR) as the example: how to apply, which settings keep your risk under control, and what to check before your first trade. Read it before you apply, so you choose the right options during the application.

Open an IBKR account → Plan your budget first

Analyst Ledger is independent and not affiliated with Interactive Brokers. Other regulated brokers work in a similar way.

1. Apply (about 10 minutes)

  1. Choose an Individual account and start the application. On IBKR's account page, click Start Application. You create a username and password, confirm your email and complete the form (IBKR).
  2. Have your details ready. Name and address, date and country of birth, tax residency and ID number, employer, income and assets, and your investing experience (what you need).
  3. Answer the experience questions honestly. They decide which products you may trade. Overstating experience gives you permissions you are not ready for.
  4. Pick your base currency. Use the currency of your salary and bank, such as EUR. Statements and margin are calculated in it.
  5. Wait for approval, then fund by bank transfer. Approval can take a few days while identity checks are done.

2. Cash or margin account

For most beginners, a cash account is the safer choice: it cannot borrow, so you cannot lose more than you deposit. A margin account lets the broker lend you money, and losses can exceed your deposit.

Cash accountMargin account
BorrowingNoYes — the broker lends against your holdings
Worst caseWhat you depositedMore than you deposited
OptionsCovered and cash-secured strategiesAll levels, subject to approval
Change laterCan upgrade to marginCan request a downgrade to cash in Settings > Account Configuration > Account Type (IBKR)

If you choose margin, size every trade by your cash, not by the larger "buying power" figure.

3. Options permissions: ask only for what you will use

IBKR has four options levels (IBKR):

  • Level 1: covered calls only.
  • Level 2: adds buying calls and puts, long spreads and collars.
  • Level 3: adds short puts and short spreads, where the loss is capped.
  • Level 4: adds naked calls and short straddles, where the loss is unlimited. Not for beginners.

Change it later in Settings > Trading > Trading Permissions > Update Options Level (IBKR guide). Leave products you do not understand, such as futures or crypto, switched off.

4. Practise first with a paper account

IBKR offers a free paper (simulated) trading account alongside the real one (IBKR). Test every new strategy there for a few weeks before using real money.

5. Currency: convert before buying US stocks

US stocks and options trade in US dollars. Convert your euros (or other currency) to dollars before you trade, so your account never runs a negative balance, which the broker treats as a loan.

  • In IBKR Mobile: Order Ticket, search EUR, choose Forex under IDEALPRO, pick EUR.USD and enter the amount (IBKR steps).
  • Orders under about USD 25,000 fill slightly worse, around 1–2 basis points outside the main quote (IBKR). The fee is 0.01% with a USD 2.50 minimum (IBKR fees), so convert in one larger order rather than many small ones.

7. Checklist before your first trade

  • Two-factor login switched on (IBKR Mobile)
  • Account type chosen on purpose: cash, or margin with a cash-only habit
  • Options level no higher than you need (Level 1–3)
  • Money converted to USD; no negative cash balance
  • Market data for what you trade (OPRA for options)
  • Strategy tested in the paper account
  • Worst case of the trade is no more than 1–2% of your account — check it in the planner
  • No results, central-bank meeting or merger news before the trade ends
  • Limit orders only, never market orders on options
  • Exit written down: profit target, stop, and the date you will close
