Bill Ackman's Pershing Square: exchanges and eye care in, Hertz out in Q3

Von Analyst Ledger research desk · 2026-11-28 · 4 Min. Lesezeit

Pershing Square's third-quarter 13F shows new stakes in Intercontinental Exchange and Alcon, a larger Netflix holding, a deep cut to Microsoft and an exit from Hertz. Reported holdings rose to about $30.8 billion.

Who they are

Bill Ackman founded Pershing Square Capital Management in New York and became known as an activist investor who takes large stakes and pushes management for change. Widely cited past campaigns include Canadian Pacific, Chipotle and Hilton. His long-running short of Herbalife is generally reported to have ended in a loss. In early 2020 a credit hedge he put on as markets began to fall was widely reported to have turned roughly $27 million into about $2.6 billion.

The firm's structure changed in 2026. Pershing Square Inc. (NYSE: PS), the parent of the investment adviser, and a new closed-end fund, Pershing Square USA (NYSE: PSUS), began trading on 29 April 2026 after a combined offering that raised $5 billion in gross proceeds, according to the company. Ackman is chairman and chief executive of the listed parent, and Ryan Israel is chief investment officer. The older Europe-listed fund, Pershing Square Holdings, continues to run alongside.

How they invest

Pershing Square runs a concentrated book of large, simple, cash-generating businesses, usually a small number of core holdings kept for years; Zhitong reports the top ten made up 86.1% of reported value at the end of September. Ackman looks for companies with durable competitive positions and is willing to engage publicly or privately with boards when he sees room for improvement. He uses options and hedges at times, and has shown he will exit quickly when a thesis breaks.

In 2026 the portfolio has shifted toward payments, exchanges and data businesses. The Motley Fool reported that Pershing exited Alphabet and Universal Music Group in the second quarter and opened positions in Visa, Mastercard and S&P Global. Ackman has also said he plans a closed-end vehicle, Pershing Square Ventures, aimed at late-stage private companies.

Latest disclosed moves

The 13F for the quarter ended 30 September 2026 was reported on 16 November. Stocktwits and a Zhitong Finance summary put reported holdings at about $30.8 billion, up from about $25.5 billion a quarter earlier, with a large line in Pershing Square Inc. itself.

  • New: Intercontinental Exchange, about 6.6 million shares (about $1.01 billion); Alcon, about 14.7 million shares (about $954 million).
  • Added: Netflix, to about 15.4 million shares from 13.08 million.
  • Exited: Hertz, fully sold.
  • Cut: Microsoft, to about 4.1 million shares, about a third fewer than in June according to Zhitong. Smaller trims to Visa, Mastercard, Meta, Brookfield and Restaurant Brands.
  • Biggest operating-company holdings: Uber (about 34.3 million shares, $2.35 billion), Amazon (about 8.5 million shares, $2.12 billion), Microsoft ($2.08 billion) and Howard Hughes Holdings (about 27.9 million shares, $2.05 billion).

How to follow them quickly

  • Form 13F: filed quarterly by Pershing Square Capital Management within 45 days of quarter-end.
  • Schedule 13D/13G: an activist stake above 5% normally triggers a 13D, now due within five business days under the 2024 rule, and it is often the first sign of a campaign.
  • Form 4: Ackman is a director, officer and 10% owner of Pershing Square Inc., so his trades in PS shares appear within two business days. An April 2026 Form 4 shows his purchases around the IPO.
  • Fund reporting: Pershing Square Holdings publishes weekly NAV and performance updates plus interim and annual reports. PS reports quarterly results with an earnings call.
  • Public statements: Ackman often explains positions on X, at investor days and on the PS earnings call.
  • EDGAR: Pershing Square Capital Management filings (CIK 0001336528).

What the filings don't show

The 13F appears up to 45 days after quarter-end, and Pershing can build or sell a position well before readers see it. The 13F lists US-listed long equities and certain options only. It omits shorts, bonds, cash, private investments and most non-US listings, so the Universal Music stake never appeared there. Hedges such as credit protection are invisible, and options are shown at the notional value of the underlying shares rather than what was paid. Activist positions can move sharply on news the market has not yet seen. Copying a filing captures neither Ackman's entry price nor his ability to influence the company.
