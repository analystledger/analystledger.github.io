Deutsche Bank Market outlook 2026-11-28

Deutsche Bank sets the Street's highest 2027 target: 9,000 for the S&P 500

Deutsche Bank's strategists expect the S&P 500 to reach about 9,000 by the end of 2027, using a framework that weighs investor demand against the supply of shares. That is 300 points above the cluster of 8,700 targets from Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and Barclays.

Why it matters: The gap with other banks comes mostly from earnings assumptions: Morgan Stanley pencils in about 14% EPS growth and Goldman about 11%. A faster slowdown in AI-driven profits is the main risk to the higher targets.

Sources: TheStreet

Goldman Sachs Flows & positioning 2026-11-25

Goldman sees $100 billion of rules-based selling this week, then a turn in early December

Goldman's trading desk estimates systematic funds sold about $70 billion of global stocks in the week to 25 November and could sell roughly $100 billion more in the following week. Trend-following funds (CTAs) have moved to a net short of about $19 billion in US stocks, and volatility-control funds have cut around $400 billion of equities since mid-October.

Why it matters: Goldman says most of the remaining selling is outside the US and expects flows to stabilise and turn into modest buying in early December. The estimate is model-based: if prices keep falling, the expected buying shrinks.

JPMorgan Market outlook 2026-11-24

JPMorgan targets 8,200 for end-2027 and flags the Fed as the biggest risk

JPMorgan's strategy team led by Dubravko Lakos-Bujas expects the S&P 500 to end 2027 at 8,200, with earnings per share around $430, about 15% growth. Its economists expect quarter-point rate hikes in December, January and March.

Why it matters: JPMorgan's target is below the 8,700 cluster despite faster earnings growth than Goldman expects, reflecting a lower valuation multiple. A hawkish Fed decision on 9 December would test that view first.

Sources: Yahoo Finance, TheStreet

Morgan Stanley Market outlook 2026-11-16

Morgan Stanley lifts its 12-month S&P 500 target to 8,700

Chief US equity strategist Mike Wilson raised the 12-month target to 8,700 from 8,000, about 15% above the 13 November close of 7,561.67. He expects the bull market to continue for a fifth year, supported by earnings growth, lower oil, steadier rate volatility and AI investment.

Why it matters: Wilson expects growth to stay healthy but slow as the first boost from cost cutting fades. The call came just before the late-November sell-off, which widened the implied upside.

Sources: CNBC
