Weekly sigma-band backtest

Put sellers often draw a ±1σ band each week and sell puts below it. This page checks, over five years of weekly moves in SPY and QQQ, how often price really stayed above the −1σ line — and how big the losing weeks were.

What this shows

  • Most weeks are calm. About 5 weeks in 6 ended above −1σ — that is why weekly put selling wins most of the time.
  • The tail is fatter than the bell curve. Falls of 3σ or more were several times more frequent than a normal distribution predicts. One such week can erase months of small gains.
  • Win rate is not profit. Always ask how big the losing weeks were, and whether the premiums collected cover them after commissions.

Method

{{ bt.method }} Moves are measured from the last close of one week to the last close of the next. Option-implied volatility is usually a little higher than realised volatility, so bands drawn from option prices are slightly wider and would be crossed somewhat less often.
